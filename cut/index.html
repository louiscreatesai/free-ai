CUT

OpenCut: the free CapCut alternative, set up in 2 minutes

OpenCut is a free, open source video editor. It's MIT licensed and has over 90,000 stars on GitHub. It runs in your browser, you don't need an account, and your videos stay on your device.

Start editing (no account)

  1. Open opencut.app and click Try early beta, then Create your first project.
  2. Click Import (or drag your clips onto the Assets panel).
  3. Drag a clip down onto the timeline. It shows up in the preview straight away.
  4. When you're done, click Export in the top right.

Try a short clip first so you know the export works on your browser before you move a full edit over.

Auto captions

  1. Click the Captions tab on the left.
  2. Leave the language on Auto detect and click Generate transcript.
  3. The first time, it downloads the caption model into your browser, so give it a minute. Then the captions land on their own track, timed to your voice.

Masks

Click your clip on the timeline, open the Masks tab on the right and click Add mask. You can pick Split, Cinematic Bars, Rectangle, Ellipse, Heart, Diamond, Star, Text or Custom. Drag the handles in the preview to move and resize it.

Speed

Open the Speed tab, type the speed you want (2 for double speed) and press Enter. There's a Change pitch switch right under it, so a sped-up voice doesn't have to sound like a chipmunk.

Good to know

  • It's still a beta. opencut.app runs the "classic" version, which the team says is the one to use today.
  • The team is rebuilding it from the ground up. The new version adds an MCP server for AI agents and a headless mode for batch rendering. It isn't out yet. It will live at new.opencut.app until it's ready.

Run it on your own computer (for developers)

The classic version runs locally with Bun and Docker. From the opencut-classic repo:

cp apps/web/.env.example apps/web/.env.local
docker compose up -d db redis serverless-redis-http
bun install
bun dev:web

Then open http://localhost:3000. Or open the repo in Claude Code and say: "Read the README and set up the web app locally for me, one step at a time."
