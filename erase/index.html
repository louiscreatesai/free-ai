ERASE: delete your data online with Claude, 6 steps and the exact prompts

What it is

You cannot vanish from the internet, and this guide does not pretend you can. The goal is smaller and doable: get your phone number, home address and private email off the places where anyone can find them in seconds. Claude does the boring part: it finds where you appear, writes the legal requests, and fills in the forms with you.

Time: about 2 hours of work plus 1 month of waiting. Cost: free, except the Chrome helper in step 5, which needs a paid Claude plan.

This guide is not legal advice. For harassment, identity theft or defamation, talk to a lawyer. Read every letter Claude writes before you send it, and never paste ID documents or passwords into a chat.

1. Find where you appear

Open a new Claude chat and paste this. Fill in the brackets with what you are comfortable sharing.

Search the web for my personal details: name [YOUR NAME], city [YOUR CITY], phone [YOUR PHONE], email [YOUR EMAIL]. Give me a table with: the exact URL, which of my details appear there, and how to contact that site to ask for removal. Look at people directories, review sites, company registers, forums, mailing lists and old profiles. Tell me honestly what you could not check.

This will not find data broker files, paid databases or anything behind a login. That is normal. Repeat this step every 6 months, because listings come back when sites rebuild their databases.

2. Send an Article 17 request (the legal lever)

In the EU and UK, Article 17 of the GDPR gives you the right to erasure. The site must answer within one month, and it can extend to three months if your request is complex. A site may refuse in a few cases, for example free speech, a legal duty to keep the data, or a legal claim. Be exact about what you want deleted. Vague requests get ignored.

Paste this into Claude, once per site:

Write a data erasure request under Article 17 of the GDPR. My name is [NAME] and you can reach me at [EMAIL]. The site is [SITE]. The exact pages are: [URLS]. Delete this data: [LIST: phone, address, email]. Mention that they must answer within one month. Ask them to confirm that they also delete backup copies and tell any partner they passed my data to. Keep it short, polite and firm. Add a line asking me to send it to the data protection officer if the site has one.

Send it to the data protection officer (DPO) if the site lists one. Keep a dated copy of everything you send.

3. Get off the phone directories (France)

If you live in France, this is the highest value step and takes about 10 minutes. Do not write to each directory. Call or write to your phone operator and ask to be put on the "liste rouge" (red list). The operator controls the source data, and within about 48 hours you disappear from the directories and reverse look-ups. A landline is listed by default. A mobile is only listed if you asked for it. If you change operator, the red list does not follow you, so ask again.

Outside France, check whether your country has the same system with your phone company.

4. Remove the Google results

Removing a result from Google is "dereferencing". The page still exists, but it no longer comes up when someone searches your name. Use Google's personal data removal form (reportcontent.google.com/forms/rtbf), give the exact URLs and prove who you are. Google replies within one month, or up to three. It weighs your privacy against the public interest. Phone numbers and addresses usually work. Press articles rarely do.

5. Let Claude fill in the forms

Install the Claude extension from the Chrome Web Store (search for Claude, made by Anthropic). It needs a paid plan (Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise). Open the removal form, ask Claude to fill it in with your details, and turn on the mode that asks you before it submits. Captchas, identity checks and email confirmations stay manual. Expect it to save you about 80 percent of the typing.

6. If they ignore you

After 30 days, send a follow-up. Paste this into Claude:

Here are my original request and the replies I got: [PASTE]. Write a polite follow-up that says the one month deadline has passed, repeats what I asked to be deleted and gives them 7 more days. Then write a short summary of the case, listing the sites by how sensitive the data is, that I can attach to a complaint.

If they still do nothing, complain to your data protection authority. In France that is the CNIL (cnil.fr), and the complaint is free. In other EU countries, use your national authority. Attach your dated copies. Many complaints to the CNIL are about exactly this right.

If you only do one thing tonight

Ask for the red list (step 3). It is free and takes ten minutes.
