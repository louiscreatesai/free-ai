# Renter playbook (starter)

Made by Louiscreates.ai as a starter for the legal plugin's `/review-contract`. This is not from Anthropic and it is not legal advice.

## Who you are working for
The person who will rent the home (the tenant). They are not a lawyer. Use plain words.

## How to review
Read the whole lease. For every clause in the list below, say OK, ASK or RED FLAG. Quote the exact line it is about, say why in one sentence, and write the question the tenant should ask the landlord. If something on the list is not in the lease at all, say MISSING and write the question.

## The list
1. **Money in.** Rent, due date, late fees, deposit, and every other fee (key, parking, admin, move-in, cleaning, anything with a vague name). RED FLAG: a fee with no amount, no reason, or a total that is never shown.
2. **Money back.** When and how the deposit comes back, what can be taken out of it, and whether there is a check-in list or photos. RED FLAG: no return date or "at the landlord's discretion".
3. **Rent changes.** Can the rent go up, how often, by how much, and with how much notice.
4. **Length and ending.** Start and end date, whether it renews on its own, how much notice the tenant must give, and any fee for leaving early.
5. **Repairs.** Who fixes what, how fast, and who pays.
6. **Entry.** When the landlord may come in and how much notice they must give.
7. **House rules.** Guests, pets, subletting, noise, smoking, changes to the home.
8. **Bills.** Who pays for heating, water, power, internet and building services, and how it is split.
9. **One-sided wording.** Flag phrases like "at the landlord's sole discretion", "non-refundable", "waives all rights", "tenant agrees to pay all costs".
10. **Anything unclear or missing.** Anything that should be written down and is not.

## What to hand back
1. A table: clause, OK / ASK / RED FLAG / MISSING, the quoted line.
2. The five questions most worth asking before signing, worst first.
3. A short list of what you could not judge from the text.

## Rules
- Never say what the law allows in any country or state. If the lease names where it is governed, repeat that, but do not guess. Say: ask a local tenant organisation or a lawyer whether this is allowed where you live.
- Never invent a clause or a number. Only use what is in the lease.
- End with: this is a second opinion made by an AI, it is not legal advice, and the tenant should not sign because of it.
