OpenWhispr: the free Wispr Flow, with the setup

What it is

OpenWhispr is a free, open-source app that types whatever you say. You press a key, talk, and your words appear wherever your cursor is: Gmail, Slack, ChatGPT, Word, any app. It also writes down your Zoom, Teams and FaceTime calls, with who said what.

Wispr Flow Pro costs $15 a month, and its free plan stops at 2,000 words a week. OpenWhispr is free and MIT licensed, and it has almost 9,000 stars on GitHub.

Get it here: github.com/OpenWhispr/openwhispr

1. Download it

Open the latest release and download the file for your computer:

  • Windows: OpenWhispr-Setup-...exe. Double-click it and follow the setup.
  • Mac with an M1, M2, M3 or M4 chip: the .dmg with arm64 in the name. Open it and drag OpenWhispr into Applications.
  • Older Intel Mac: the .dmg without arm64.
  • Linux: the .deb (Ubuntu, Debian) or the .AppImage.

2. Pick a local model so it runs free and offline

When the app asks how to transcribe, pick a local model (NVIDIA Parakeet or Whisper). It downloads once, then everything runs on your own computer. That means it works with no internet, and your audio never leaves your device.

You can also plug in a cloud model with your own key, but you don't need one.

3. Press the key and talk

Click into any text box, press your dictation hotkey (the app shows it), and talk like you're texting a friend. Stop, and the text lands at your cursor.

4. Turn on meeting notes

Start a Zoom, Teams or FaceTime call and OpenWhispr picks it up. It writes the whole call down and labels who said what, and your notes stay searchable later.

Honest notes

  • The first local model download takes a few minutes and some disk space.
  • On a Mac you'll be asked to allow the microphone, and to let the app type into other apps.
  • The cloud features and team spaces need a sign-in. The local dictation doesn't.
