20 style lines that make ChatGPT posters look designed

ChatGPT's new Sketch tool, part of ChatGPT Images 2.5, turns a quick doodle into an image. Here's how to use it, plus 20 lines to paste in so your poster looks designed rather than generated.

How to use Sketch (on your phone)

  1. Open the ChatGPT app, type @ in the message box and pick Sketch.
  2. Draw your idea. Stick figures are fine: the shapes and layout are what matter.
  3. Describe the poster, then paste one style line from below. Put any words you want on the poster in quotes, and keep them short.

Image generation has daily limits (lower on free accounts), so start with your best idea.

The trick

A blog post that went viral on Hacker News, "AI-generated posters don't have to be horrible" (john.hartnup.uk), found that ChatGPT slides into bland pastel, airbrushed looks unless you steer it. So: - Avoid: pastel, airbrush, oil painting, "cute", "whimsical". - Name a real print style or design movement, a limited colour palette, and lots of empty space.

The 20 lines (copy one, paste it after your description)

  1. Risograph print poster, two ink colours (fluorescent pink and blue), visible grain, slightly misaligned layers.
  2. Swiss International Style poster, strict grid, one bold sans-serif headline, lots of white space, one red accent.
  3. Bauhaus poster, primary colours, simple geometric shapes, asymmetric layout, heavy black type.
  4. 1960s screen print, three flat colours, halftone texture, bold silhouette shapes.
  5. Japanese minimal poster, one striking image, huge negative space, small vertical type.
  6. Letterpress poster, wood type in two colours, uneven ink, cream paper.
  7. Cut paper collage, torn edges, flat bold colours, playful layout.
  8. Brutalist poster, oversized black type, raw grid, one neon accent colour.
  9. Memphis design poster, squiggles and confetti shapes, bold clashing colours, thick black outlines.
  10. Photocopied zine flyer, black and white, high contrast, rough edges, hand-cut text.
  11. Art Deco travel poster, symmetrical composition, gold and deep teal, elegant geometric lettering.
  12. Mid-century modern poster, muted orange and olive, simple shapes, textured paper.
  13. Constructivist poster, diagonal composition, red and black, bold block type.
  14. Minimal line-art poster, one continuous black line drawing on off-white, tiny caption.
  15. Vintage national-park style poster, flat layered landscape colours, bold serif title.
  16. Duotone photo poster, one high-contrast image in two colours, big headline across the middle.
  17. Blueprint poster, white technical lines on deep blue, labels and measurements.
  18. Stencil street poster, spray-paint texture, two colours, rough and bold.
  19. Swiss punk poster, broken grid, overlapping type, black, white and one fluorescent colour.
  20. Children's picture-book poster, flat shapes, limited palette of three colours, soft paper texture.

Worth knowing

  • Keep the words on the poster short. Long text is where AI images still slip up.
  • Don't use brand logos or living artists' names; describe the style instead.
  • If you post it anywhere, say it was made with AI.
