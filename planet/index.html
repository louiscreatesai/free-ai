PLANET: look at the same free NASA telescope data

The story

A developer used Claude Code (and other AI tools) on NASA telescope data and flagged a possible planet around the star TIC 4206066. It would be about 1.4 times the size of Earth and circle its star about every 3 days. The star dims for about two hours on that rhythm.

Nothing is confirmed. NASA's TESS telescope is expected to look at that spot in November. The developer also says he hid part of the data and Claude Code correctly predicted when the star would dim in the gap. Treat all of it as a candidate, not a discovery.

The data is free

TESS data is public. NASA's archive has no proprietary period, so anyone can download the light curves (how bright a star is over time). The home of the data is the MAST archive.

Look at it with Claude Code

  1. Install Python and Claude Code.
  2. Ask Claude Code: Install the lightkurve package. Download the TESS light curve for TIC 4206066 and plot it. Explain what I am looking at.
  3. The lightkurve docs explain the tool. Its quickstart shows a Kepler example, so ask Claude to adapt it to TESS and to show you the search results before it downloads anything.
  4. Ask: What would a planet crossing this star look like? Show me where those dips would be.
  5. Try another star. You can look up any TESS target on MAST by its TIC number.

Honest limits

  • Most dips are not planets. Pairs of stars that eclipse each other, star spots and instrument noise all look similar.
  • A candidate is a hint. Real confirmation takes follow-up measurements by professionals.
  • Do not announce a discovery. If you think you found something, community candidates are tracked by professionals at ExoFOP-TESS.

  • The AI can sound sure and be wrong. Ask it to try to disprove its own finding.

  • Sources: XDA Developers, MAST archive
